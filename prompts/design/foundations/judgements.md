# The judgements

Everything in the base serves these six. When a rule and a judgement conflict, the judgement
wins. When two judgements conflict, the earlier one wins.

<!-- section: ground-in-subject -->
## 1. Ground it in the subject

Every distinctive choice comes from the subject's own world — its materials, instruments,
vernacular, units and conventions — not from a mood board of other interfaces. A cosy
fishing game's menu smells of rope and tide tables; a lab-sim HUD reads like an instrument
panel. Carry at least one detail only this subject would have, **as content**, not ornament:
its real units, its terms of art, its document conventions. The failure this prevents is the
interchangeable screen: competent, and belonging to nothing.

Pin the subject before anything else: one concrete thing, its audience, and the surface's
single job. If the job is not clear, the design cannot be judged, only liked.

<!-- section: density -->
## 2. Density is a feature where people work; air is a feature where they arrive

A tool used for hours defaults to 13 px on a 4 px grid and spends space only where scanning
breaks down. A landing page, a title screen or a hero is seen once and earns air. The
failure either way is the same: whitespace that reads as "designed" in a screenshot and as
"scrolling" on the fourth hour, or a title screen packed like a settings dialog.

<!-- section: one-accent -->
## 3. One accent, used sparingly

The accent marks the one action or the one thing that matters on this screen. A second
accent does not add emphasis; it removes it — when two things are highlighted, neither is.
Semantic colours (ok, warn, error) are not accents and are never borrowed for emphasis. In a
game the accent is also a gameplay signal: pickups, the goal, the interactable — so it must
never be spent on chrome.

<!-- section: hairlines-over-shadows -->
## 4. Hairlines over shadows, in the layout

A shadow on a surface that sits *in* the page invents a light source the flat surfaces
around it contradict. Shadows are for things that float *over* the page — menus, dialogs,
tooltips — where lift is the actual message. Borders, fills, radius and shadow each say
"separate object"; spend them by role, and lift only the thing that needs lifting.

<!-- section: motion-means-something -->
## 5. Motion must mean something

Every animation answers "what changed, and where did it come from". Decoration that
survives a second viewing is rare; decoration that survives the four-hundredth is rarer. In
a game the same test applies to juice: a screen shake that fires on every hit is noise by the
tenth minute.

<!-- section: state-never-colour-alone -->
## 6. State is never colour alone

Every state carries a second signal — a glyph, a label, a position, a shape. Eight percent
of men are colour-blind, every player is colour-blind in bright sunlight, and every HUD is
viewed on a TV whose colour nobody calibrated.

<!-- section: hierarchy -->
## The composition rules that follow from these

1. **One focal point.** Decide what the eye lands on first and subordinate everything else.
   Two focal points is none.
2. **Three levels of hierarchy, no more.** Primary, secondary, tertiary. A fourth is
   invisible in practice.
3. **Align to something.** Every edge shares an axis with another edge. Optical alignment
   beats mathematical alignment where they disagree — icons and 3D silhouettes especially.
4. **Repeat, don't invent.** The fourth card, the fourth crate, the fourth menu row looks
   exactly like the first. Novelty per instance is what makes a thing feel assembled.
5. **Whitespace is structure.** Grouping by spacing beats grouping by border.
6. **Contrast carries meaning.** If two things look different they must *be* different.
7. **Limit the palette per screen.** Neutrals, one accent, at most one semantic colour.
8. **Size by importance, not by content.** The largest element is the most important one.
9. **Every state, every time.** Empty, loading, partial, error, full.
10. **Reduce until it breaks, then add one thing back.** The only reliable route to minimal
    that still works.
